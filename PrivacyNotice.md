## 1. About this notice

1.1 This notice explains how personal data is processed in connection with your use of a Work, as clause 3 of our Terms of Use defines that word.

1.2 This notice is written to comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), referred to in this notice as the "GDPR."

1.3 This notice supplements clause 19 of our Terms of Use. Clause 19 states the facts. This notice explains what those facts mean for your rights under the GDPR.

---

## 2. Why we publish this notice, even though we are not a company

2.1 We are independent developers. We are not a company. Clause 4 of our Terms of Use describes how we work.

2.2 The GDPR can apply to processing of personal data even where there is no company, if a person responsible for the processing is established in the European Union, or if the processing relates to offering a service to people in the European Union.

2.3 We publish this notice as a matter of transparency and good practice, whether or not the GDPR strictly requires it of any particular Contributor.

---

## 3. Who is responsible for your data

3.1 No Contributor operates a server that receives or stores data about you. No Contributor runs user accounts. No Contributor asks you to register. No Contributor asks you for personal information.

3.2 Because of this, for almost all of the processing described in this notice, no Contributor decides the purpose or the means of that processing. Under the GDPR, this means no Contributor is the "controller" of that data. The person who does decide the purpose and the means of that processing is named in each section below.

3.3 No Contributor has appointed a data protection officer. Given the nature and the scale of the processing described in this notice, we do not believe one is required. We will revisit this if that changes.

3.4 For a question about this notice, or about data protection, contact privacy@stability.nexus.

---

## 4. Our websites

4.1 Our websites are hosted by a third party, currently GitHub Pages.

4.2 That host receives technical connection data about you, such as your IP address, your browser type, and the page you requested. That host processes this data as an independent controller, under its own privacy policy, currently GitHub's own privacy statement, at https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement.

4.3 No Contributor receives, stores, or has access to the connection data described in clause 4.2.

4.4 We do not use analytics, advertising, or tracking scripts on our websites, beyond what our host applies to every site it hosts.

---

## 5. Our Interfaces

5.1 An Interface is a static web page or mobile app, described in clause 13 of our Terms of Use. It runs in your browser. No Contributor operates a server behind it.

5.2 An Interface may store data on your own device, for example in your browser's local storage, so that it can remember your settings between visits. This data stays on your device. No Contributor receives it, stores it, or has access to it.

5.3 An Interface reads public data from a blockchain. It does this directly from your own browser. No Contributor is in the path of that request.

5.4 An Interface contacts third-party endpoints so that it can work, for example an RPC provider, a wallet connector, or a block explorer. Clause 19.4 of our Terms of Use explains this, and the source code of each Interface names its default endpoints.

5.5 Those endpoints receive your IP address, and may receive your wallet address, directly from your browser. Each endpoint processes this data as an independent controller, under its own privacy policy. No Contributor receives, stores, or has access to this data. Clause 15 of our Terms of Use explains that a third-party service has its own terms and its own privacy policy, and that they apply to you, not to us.

5.6 If an Interface lets you choose your own endpoint, for example your own RPC provider or your own wallet, the data described in clause 5.5 goes to the endpoint you chose, not to a default endpoint.

---

## 6. Public blockchains

6.1 If you send a transaction to a Protocol, that transaction is processed by the blockchain's own validators, not by any Contributor. Clause 5.2 and clause 12.1 of our Terms of Use explain this.

6.2 A blockchain transaction is public and permanent. It typically includes your wallet address, and it may include other data that you chose to include. Clause 19.7 of our Terms of Use explains that a wallet address can be linked to your identity, and that nobody, including us, can delete blockchain data.

6.3 No Contributor decides whether your transaction data is recorded, how long it is kept, or who can see it. We do not believe any Contributor is a controller of this data, once it is recorded on the blockchain. The validators of the blockchain, who are not Contributors, are the persons who process it.

6.4 Because nobody controls a public blockchain once data is recorded on it, the rights described in section 8 of this notice, in particular the right to erasure and the right to rectification, cannot be exercised over data that is already recorded on a blockchain. No Contributor can change this. Do not put personal data into a transaction that you send to a Protocol, beyond what the Protocol's own design requires.

## 7. Why this data is processed, and on what basis

7.1 The connection data described in clause 4.2 is processed by our host so that it can deliver our websites to your browser. This is necessary for our host to provide its hosting service.

7.2 The endpoint data described in clause 5.5 is processed by each endpoint so that your transaction or your query can reach a blockchain, at your own initiative. This is necessary to perform the action that you asked an Interface to take.

7.3 Blockchain data described in section 6 is processed by a blockchain's validators so that the blockchain can operate, and so that your transaction can be recorded and verified. This happens because you chose to send that transaction.

7.4 We do not process any of the data described in this notice for our own purposes. We do not use it for marketing, for profiling, or for automated decision-making that produces a legal or similarly significant effect on you.

---

## 8. Your rights

8.1 Where the GDPR applies, you have the right to ask a controller for access to your personal data, for correction of inaccurate data, for erasure of your data, for restriction of processing, for a copy of your data in a portable format, and to object to processing.

8.2 For the connection data described in section 4, and the endpoint data described in section 5, address these rights to the host or the endpoint concerned, since a Contributor does not control that data. Their own privacy policy explains how.

8.3 For blockchain data described in section 6, clause 6.4 of this notice explains why these rights cannot practically be exercised over data already recorded on a blockchain, and why no Contributor can exercise them on your behalf.

8.4 You have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority. Because no Contributor has a registered office in the European Union, there may be no single lead authority for a complaint about us. If you live in the European Union, you may complain to the supervisory authority of the country where you live, where you work, or where you believe an infringement took place.

## 9. International transfers

9.1 The connection data, endpoint data, and blockchain data described in this notice may be processed in any country, since our host, the operators of third-party endpoints, and the validators of a blockchain may be located anywhere in the world.

9.2 No Contributor transfers your data internationally, because no Contributor receives or stores it. Any international transfer happens within systems that a Contributor does not operate.

---

## 10. Retention

10.1 We do not decide how long the connection data described in section 4, or the endpoint data described in section 5, is kept. Each host or endpoint decides this under its own privacy policy.

10.2 Blockchain data described in section 6 is kept permanently, for as long as the blockchain continues to operate. Nobody, including us, can change this.

The security of the systems described in this notice, the host, the endpoints, and the blockchain, is a matter for the party that operates each of them.
